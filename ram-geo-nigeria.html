Nigeria — Study Guide

From Ram's Nigeria advanced readings (Nov 6, 2026)

Study notes

  • Nigeria gained full independence from Britain in October 1960, as a federation of three regions — northern, western, and eastern — under a parliamentary form of government.
  • In October 1963 Nigeria proclaimed itself a federal republic, ending its remaining constitutional tie to the British Crown.
  • Nigeria is the most populous country in Africa and accounts for over half of West Africa's population (the fact sheet lists 168 million, its January 2012 figure).
  • It lies on the Gulf of Guinea, between Benin to the west and Cameroon to the east.
  • The capital is Abuja.
  • Major natural resources: petroleum, natural gas, tin, iron ore, coal, limestone, niobium, lead, zinc, and arable land.
  • Natural hazards include periodic droughts and flooding.
  • Nigeria has about 250 ethnic groups. The largest are the Hausa and Fulani (29%), Yoruba (21%), Igbo — also spelled Ibo (18%), Ijaw (10%), Kanuri (4%), Ibibio (3.5%), and Tiv (2.5%).
  • English is the official language; Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, and Fulani are widely spoken, along with over 500 additional indigenous languages.
  • The Hausa-Fulani dominate the northern two-thirds of the country and are mostly Muslim; the Nupe, Tiv, and Kanuri are other major northern groups.
  • The Yoruba predominate in the southwest and the Igbo in the southeast.
  • The population is roughly balanced between Muslims and Christians, with a small minority practising indigenous beliefs.
  • From the set's maps & readings: Abuja is a purpose-built capital in a central location, chosen to sit between the country's regions; it replaced Lagos as capital in 1991.
  • From the set's maps & readings: Lagos, on the Gulf of Guinea coast, is Nigeria's largest city and its former capital, and remains its main port and commercial centre.
  • From the set's maps & readings: The Niger and Benue rivers meet at Lokoja — the great confluence that splits Nigeria's drainage into its northern and southern reaches.
  • From the set's maps & readings: South of the confluence the Niger fans out into the Niger Delta, the oil-rich wetland region where the river empties into the Gulf of Guinea.
  • From the set's maps & readings: Lake Chad occupies Nigeria's far northeastern corner and is shared with Chad, Niger, and Cameroon; it has shrunk dramatically over recent decades.
  • From the set's maps & readings: Nigeria's historic kingdoms and empires — among them Kanem-Bornu in the northeast, the Hausa city-states and later the Sokoto Caliphate in the north, and the Oyo Empire and Benin Kingdom in the south — shaped the regions that became the modern country.
  • From the set's maps & readings: Kano, in the north, is one of the old Hausa city-states and a historic trading city on trans-Saharan routes.
  • From the set's maps & readings: The stretch of coast along the Bight of Benin was known as the Slave Coast, a major departure region of the Atlantic slave trade.
  • From the set's maps & readings: Cross River National Park, in the southeast near Cameroon, protects some of Nigeria's last rainforest and is home to the rare Cross River gorilla.
  • From the set's maps & readings: Chappal Waddi is Nigeria's highest point at 2,419 m; it rises in Gashaka-Gumti National Park in Taraba State.
  • From the set's maps & readings: The Jos Plateau, in central Nigeria, is a high, cooler upland long known for tin mining.
  • From the set's maps & readings: Zuma Rock is the striking solitary rock that stands beside the Abuja–Kaduna road just outside the capital.

Traps & confusables

  • Abuja vs Lagos: Abuja is the capital (since 1991); Lagos is the largest city and the former capital. Questions asking for the "largest city" want Lagos, not the capital.
  • Niger vs Nigeria: Niger is a separate, landlocked country to the north (capital Niamey). Nigeria is the coastal country on the Gulf of Guinea.
  • Niger State is also the name of one of Nigeria's own states — and the Niger Delta, far to the south, is neither Niger State nor the country Niger.
  • The Niger River flows through the country Niger, then Nigeria, before reaching the sea in the Niger Delta — the river, the country, the state, and the delta are four different things.
  • Benin vs Benin City: the Republic of Benin is Nigeria's western neighbour; Benin City and the historic Benin Kingdom are inside Nigeria.
  • Igbo is the same people older sources spell "Ibo" — both spellings appear in the fact sheet.
  • The capital moved for centrality and neutrality: Abuja was purpose-built in the middle of the country, not grown from an older port city like Lagos.

Self-test

Q1. What is the capital of Nigeria, and what kind of capital is it?

Answer

Abuja — a purpose-built capital in a central location; it became the capital in 1991.

Q2. Which Nigerian city is the country's largest, and what role did it hold before 1991?

Answer

Lagos — it was the capital until Abuja replaced it in 1991, and it remains the main port and commercial centre.

Q3. At which town do the Niger and Benue rivers join?

Answer

Lokoja, at the great confluence in central Nigeria.

Q4. What is Nigeria's highest point, how high is it, and where is it found?

Answer

Chappal Waddi, 2,419 m, in Gashaka-Gumti National Park in Taraba State.

Q5. In what year did Nigeria gain independence, and in what year did it become a republic?

Answer

Independence in October 1960; it proclaimed itself a federal republic in October 1963.

Q6. Which ethnic group predominates in Nigeria's southwest, and which in its southeast?

Answer

The Yoruba in the southwest and the Igbo (Ibo) in the southeast; the Hausa-Fulani dominate the north.

Q7. What is Nigeria's official language?

Answer

English — though over 500 indigenous languages are also spoken, including Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, and Fulani.

Q8. Which lake in Nigeria's northeast is shared with three other countries?

Answer

Lake Chad, shared with Chad, Niger, and Cameroon.

Q9. What is the name of the oil-rich region where the Niger River reaches the Gulf of Guinea?

Answer

The Niger Delta.

Q10. Which national park protects rainforest and the Cross River gorilla?

Answer

Cross River National Park, in the southeast near the Cameroon border.

Q11. What was the historic name for Nigeria's Bight of Benin coast during the Atlantic slave trade era?

Answer

The Slave Coast.

Q12. What is the famous solitary rock standing beside the road just outside Abuja?

Answer

Zuma Rock.
