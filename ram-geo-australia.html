Australia — Study Guide

From Ram's Australia readings & maps

Study notes

  • Kakadu National Park: a major national park in Australia's Northern Territory, next to Arnhem Land — Ram's note stresses it is in Australia, not Africa.
  • Arnhem Land: over 94,000 km² of Aboriginal territory in the Northern Territory, lying between Kakadu National Park, the Arafura Sea and the Gulf of Carpentaria, in Australia's tropical north.
  • Darwin: the Northern Territory capital is NOT in Arnhem Land, though it is the access point for Kakadu and Arnhem Land.
  • Dundas Strait: the waterway at the tip of the Cobourg Peninsula (Northern Territory), between the peninsula and Melville Island, on the important shipping route to Darwin.
  • Cape Don Light: the lighthouse on Cape Don, at the tip of the Cobourg Peninsula inside Garig Gunak Barlu National Park, marks the entrance to the Dundas Strait; it is Australia's northernmost traditional lighthouse.
  • Mount Kosciuszko: Australia's tallest peak, standing in the Snowy Mountains inside Kosciuszko National Park (New South Wales).
  • Australian Alps: the mountain system spanning New South Wales, the Australian Capital Territory and Victoria, embracing 16 national parks and reserves; the Snowy Mountains form part of it.
  • Snowy River: one of Australia's largest snowmelt rivers — it rises in the Snowy Mountains (Kosciuszko National Park, NSW) and flows to the sea at Marlo in East Gippsland, Victoria.
  • Snowy River National Park: in Victoria, about 390 km north-east of Melbourne — despite the river's NSW headwaters, this park is Victorian.
  • Jervis Bay Territory: a small Commonwealth territory (about 6,700 hectares / 75.5 km²) on the southern shores of Jervis Bay, roughly 200 km south of Sydney; transferred from New South Wales to the federal government in 1915 to give the landlocked ACT a seaport, and kept as a separate federal territory when the ACT gained self-government in 1988.
  • From the set's maps: the Great Artesian Basin is the world's largest artesian basin, a vast underground water source lying beneath about 22% of Australia (much of the dry interior of Queensland, NSW, South Australia and the NT), tapped by bores for inland farms and towns.
  • From the set's maps: the Jervis Bay Territory is the Australian Capital Territory's coastal exclave — a patch of federal territory on the NSW coast, home to the naval base HMAS Creswell, so the landlocked capital territory has a port.
  • From the set's maps: the Murray–Darling Basin is Australia's largest river system — the Murray River and its great tributary the Darling together drain about one-seventh of the continent across NSW, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and the ACT, and form the country's main agricultural food bowl.

Traps & confusables

  • Kakadu is Australian: Kakadu National Park is in the Northern Territory — a classic slip is placing it in Africa.
  • Darwin vs Arnhem Land: Darwin gives access to Arnhem Land but is not part of it.
  • Mount Kosciuszko vs Kosciuszko National Park vs Snowy Mountains: the peak is Mount Kosciuszko; the park named after it (Kosciuszko National Park) surrounds it; the Snowy Mountains are the wider range holding both.
  • Dundas Strait location: it separates the Cobourg Peninsula from Melville Island in the Northern Territory — not a strait in Western Australia or Queensland.
  • Jervis Bay's status: it is its own federal territory, not part of New South Wales (which surrounds it) and not part of the ACT (which it was created to serve).
  • Snowy River National Park: Victorian, even though the Snowy River starts in New South Wales.

Self-test

Q1. Which national park lies beside Arnhem Land in the Northern Territory — and on which continent is it found?

Answer

Kakadu National Park — in Australia (not Africa).

Q2. The Dundas Strait separates the Cobourg Peninsula from which island?

Answer

Melville Island (Northern Territory).

Q3. What is the name of Australia's northernmost traditional lighthouse, and what does it mark?

Answer

Cape Don Light — it marks the entrance to the Dundas Strait, on the route to Darwin.

Q4. What is Australia's tallest peak, and in which mountain range and national park does it stand?

Answer

Mount Kosciuszko — in the Snowy Mountains, inside Kosciuszko National Park (NSW).

Q5. The Snowy River rises in the Snowy Mountains and reaches the sea at which Victorian town/region?

Answer

At Marlo, in East Gippsland, Victoria.

Q6. Why was the Jervis Bay Territory transferred from New South Wales to the federal government in 1915?

Answer

To give the landlocked Australian Capital Territory a seaport; it remained a separate Commonwealth territory after ACT self-government in 1988.

Q7. What is the world's largest artesian basin, and roughly how much of Australia does it underlie?

Answer

The Great Artesian Basin — beneath about 22% of Australia.

Q8. Australia's largest river system is formed by which two rivers?

Answer

The Murray and the Darling (the Murray–Darling Basin).

Q9. The Jervis Bay Territory is best described as an exclave of which Australian territory?

Answer

The Australian Capital Territory — its coastal exclave on the NSW coast (site of the HMAS Creswell naval base).

Q10. True or false: Darwin is the main town inside Arnhem Land.

Answer

False — Darwin is not in Arnhem Land; it is the access point for Kakadu and Arnhem Land.
