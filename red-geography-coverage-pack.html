Red Set Geography Study Guide

How this guide works

This guide covers the scope of the IAC 2026-27 Red Set Geography Study Guide: its six regional sections first, then all 33 Terms for Further Study. The official guide warns that most tournament questions are not referenced in it at all. Treat this document as the floor, not the ceiling.

Every topic follows the same chain:

  1. Identify - what the thing is, in one line.
  2. Locate - where it is, what it borders, and what larger unit it belongs to.
  3. Explain - the key facts a question can be built from.
  4. Significance - why it matters enough to be asked about.
  5. Connections - the nearby topics it links to.
  6. Confusables - the trap answer, and how to tell the two apart under time pressure.

African Geography

The Niger River

  • Identify: The Niger is the third-longest river in Africa, after the Nile and the Congo, at roughly 4,180 kilometres.
  • Locate: It rises in the Guinea highlands near the Sierra Leone border, flows north-east into Mali, bends south-east through Niger, then crosses Nigeria and empties into the Gulf of Guinea through a vast delta.
  • Explain: Its strangest feature is the Inland Delta in Mali, where the river spreads into a seasonal maze of lakes, marshes and channels around Mopti instead of flowing in one channel. Downstream it passes Niamey, the capital of Niger, and is joined by its great tributary the Benue at Lokoja in Nigeria. Timbuktu sits near the river's northern bend in Mali.
  • Significance: The river waters the farmland of three countries and its delta in Nigeria is the country's oil region, so it anchors questions on West African trade, cities and resources.
  • Connections: Links to the Sahel (the belt it crosses), to Timbuktu and Djenne, and to Nigeria's oil delta.
  • Confusables: Niger River vs Niger vs Nigeria. Niger (with a soft "zh" sound in careful speech) is the landlocked country whose capital is Niamey. Nigeria is the larger, more populous coastal country whose capital is Abuja and largest city Lagos. The river flows through both, and it shares its name with the country Niger - that shared name is exactly the fact the official guide flags. If the clue mentions the Inland Delta or Timbuktu, it is the river. If it mentions Abuja or Lagos, it is Nigeria. If it mentions Niamey as a capital, it is the country Niger.

Mount Kilimanjaro and Tanzania

  • Identify: Mount Kilimanjaro is a dormant stratovolcano and, at 5,895 metres, the tallest mountain in Africa and the tallest free-standing mountain in the world.
  • Locate: It stands in north-eastern Tanzania, close to the border with Kenya. Its highest point, Uhuru Peak, sits on the rim of its tallest cone, Kibo; the other cones are Mawenzi and Shira.
  • Explain: "Free-standing" means it rises alone from the surrounding plains rather than as one peak in a range, which is why its snow-capped summit looks so dramatic above the savanna. The town of Moshi is the usual gateway for climbers.
  • Significance: It is Africa's highest point and one of the Seven Summits, so it appears in any superlative question about African mountains.
  • Connections: Links to Tanzania (whose capital is Dodoma and largest city Dar es Salaam), to Lake Victoria on Tanzania's north-west border, and to the Serengeti in its north.
  • Confusables: Mount Kenya, in Kenya, is Africa's second-highest mountain at 5,199 metres - higher than everything except Kilimanjaro, but not the answer when "tallest in Africa" is asked. Also separate the mountain (Kilimanjaro) from the country (Tanzania): a question asking where Africa's tallest mountain stands wants Tanzania only if it asks for the country.

Asian Geography

The Philippines and Manila

  • Identify: The Philippines is a Southeast Asian archipelago country of more than 7,000 islands, with its capital at Manila.
  • Locate: It lies in the western Pacific, east of Vietnam across the South China Sea and north of Indonesia. The three main island groups are Luzon in the north, the Visayas in the centre and Mindanao in the south.
  • Explain: Manila stands on Manila Bay on the south-western coast of Luzon, the largest island, which also holds Quezon City, the country's most populous city. Other major islands include Palawan, Cebu, Panay and Negros. The country sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, so volcanoes such as Mayon and Taal and frequent typhoons are standard clue material.
  • Significance: It is one of the world's great archipelago states, and questions use it to test island groups, capitals and Ring of Fire geography.
  • Connections: Links to Borneo (just south-west of Palawan), to the South China Sea disputes, and to Luzon and Mindanao as separate answers in their own right.
  • Confusables: Indonesia is the even larger archipelago to the south, with its capital at Jakarta on Java. Manila, Luzon and Mindanao always point to the Philippines; Jakarta, Sumatra and Java point to Indonesia.

The Yangtze River

  • Identify: The Yangtze is the longest river in China and in Asia, and the third-longest river in the world, at about 6,300 kilometres.
  • Locate: It rises on the Tibetan Plateau in Qinghai, flows east across central China through Chongqing, Wuhan and Nanjing, and reaches the East China Sea at Shanghai.
  • Explain: The Three Gorges Dam, the world's largest power station by installed capacity, spans it in Hubei province. Its fertile delta around Shanghai is China's economic engine room.
  • Significance: "Longest river in China" and "longest river in Asia" are both the Yangtze, making it one of the most reliable superlative answers in Asian geography.
  • Connections: Links to Shanghai at its mouth, to the Three Gorges Dam, and to the Grand Canal, which crosses it.
  • Confusables: The Yellow River (Huang He) is China's second-longest river and flows much farther north, looping through the loess country whose yellow silt gives it its name and its colour. Loess, silt and "China's Sorrow" mean Yellow River. Three Gorges, Chongqing and Shanghai mean Yangtze.

Bangkok and the Chao Phraya River

  • Identify: Bangkok is the capital and largest city of Thailand, and it stands in the delta of the Chao Phraya River.
  • Locate: The Chao Phraya flows south through central Thailand, through the old capital Ayutthaya and then Bangkok, and empties into the Gulf of Thailand a short distance below the city.
  • Explain: The river is formed where the Ping, Wang, Yom and Nan rivers join at Nakhon Sawan, and it waters the rice-growing central plain that feeds the country. Bangkok grew up on its banks and canals - the canals, called khlongs, are a standard clue. Bangkok's full ceremonial name begins with the words Krung Thep.
  • Significance: Capital-plus-river pairings are a favourite question shape, and Bangkok on the Chao Phraya is the Southeast Asian example most likely to be asked.
  • Connections: Links to Ayutthaya upstream, to the Gulf of Thailand, and to Thailand's neighbours Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia and Malaysia.
  • Confusables: The Mekong is the giant river of mainland Southeast Asia, but it does not flow through Bangkok - it forms Thailand's border with Laos and crosses Cambodia and Vietnam. If the capital is in the clue, the river is the Chao Phraya.

Jerusalem

  • Identify: Jerusalem is a city in the Judaean hills considered holy to Judaism, Islam and Christianity.
  • Locate: It stands inland, roughly midway between the Mediterranean Sea to the west and the Dead Sea to the east, in the hill country of Israel and the West Bank.
  • Explain: Its walled Old City holds the Western Wall and the Temple Mount (with the Dome of the Rock and al-Aqsa Mosque) for Judaism and Islam, and the Church of the Holy Sepulchre for Christianity.
  • Significance: "Holy to three religions" is one of the most distinctive clue phrases in all of geography, and it points to only one city.
  • Connections: Links to the Dead Sea (the lowest land on Earth, to its east), to Bethlehem just south, and to Tel Aviv on the coast.
  • Confusables: Tel Aviv is Israel's coastal economic centre and the city most foreign embassies historically used, but it has no holy-city status. Mecca is holy to Islam alone. Three faiths in one clue always means Jerusalem.

Borneo

  • Identify: Borneo is the third-largest island in the world and the largest island in Asia.
  • Locate: It sits in maritime Southeast Asia, astride the equator, surrounded by the South China Sea, the Java Sea and the Makassar Strait.
  • Explain: Unusually, one island is shared by three countries: Indonesia takes the largest share, called Kalimantan, in the south and centre; Malaysia holds Sabah and Sarawak in the north; and the small oil-rich sultanate of Brunei is tucked into the north-west coast between them. It holds some of the world's oldest rainforest, the orangutan's main home, and Mount Kinabalu, Malaysia's highest peak.
  • Significance: "Island shared by three countries" is a near-unique fingerprint, and Borneo is also a standard answer for rainforest, orangutan and equator questions.
  • Connections: Links to the Philippines (across the Sulu Sea), to Brunei as a separate answer, and to the Congo and Amazon rainforests as the world's other great rainforest blocks.
  • Confusables: Sumatra, Java, Sulawesi and New Guinea are the other great Indonesian islands, but each belongs to one country (New Guinea is shared by two). A three-country split can only be Borneo.

Istanbul and the Hagia Sophia

  • Identify: Istanbul is the largest city of Turkey, and the Hagia Sophia is its most famous building.
  • Locate: Istanbul straddles the Bosporus strait, so part of the city is in Europe and part in Asia. The Hagia Sophia stands in the old city on the European side, near the Topkapi Palace and facing the Blue Mosque.
  • Explain: The city was founded as Byzantium, renamed Constantinople under the Romans, and took the name Istanbul after the Ottoman conquest of 1453. The Hagia Sophia was completed in 537 CE under the emperor Justinian as a cathedral, served as a mosque under the Ottomans, then as a museum, and is a mosque again today; its vast dome was an engineering marvel for a thousand years.
  • Significance: Istanbul is the classic "city on two continents" answer, and the Hagia Sophia is the classic Byzantine architecture answer.
  • Connections: Links directly to the Bosporus entry in the European section, to Ankara (Turkey's capital), and to the Black Sea and Sea of Marmara at either end of the strait.
  • Confusables: Ankara is Turkey's capital - Istanbul is only its largest city, and "capital of Turkey" must never be buzzed as Istanbul. For the building, the Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque) with its six minarets stands across the square; the 6th-century dome built by Justinian is the Hagia Sophia.

Australia and Oceania Geography

The Great Barrier Reef

  • Identify: The Great Barrier Reef is the world's largest coral reef system and the largest structure on Earth made by living organisms.
  • Locate: It stretches for more than 2,300 kilometres along the coast of Queensland in north-eastern Australia, in the Coral Sea.
  • Explain: It is not one reef but roughly 3,000 individual reefs and 900 islands, built over thousands of years by tiny coral polyps laying down limestone. It is a World Heritage site off towns such as Cairns, and it is under pressure from warming water and coral bleaching.
  • Significance: "Largest structure made by living things" is the official guide's exact framing, and it is a giveaway phrase for this answer.
  • Connections: Links to Queensland, to the Coral Sea, and to Australia itself - the largest country of Oceania.
  • Confusables: The Belize Barrier Reef and the Mesoamerican reef system are the largest in the Americas but far smaller. Any superlative about size, or a Queensland location, means the Great Barrier Reef.

Antarctica

  • Identify: Antarctica is Earth's southernmost continent, centred on the geographic South Pole.
  • Locate: It is ringed by the Southern Ocean and lies south of South America, Africa and Australia, roughly equally distant from all three at its edges.
  • Explain: It is the coldest, driest, windiest and highest continent on average, and its ice sheet holds most of the world's fresh water in frozen form. No country owns it: the Antarctic Treaty reserves it for peaceful science, and its only residents staff research stations such as McMurdo Station (see Terms).
  • Significance: It completes the set of seven continents and anchors every "southernmost" and superlative-climate question.
  • Connections: Links to McMurdo Station, the Weddell Sea and the South Pole station in the trainer bank.
  • Confusables: The Arctic is not a continent at all - it is an ocean covered in sea ice around the North Pole, surrounded by land. South Pole, continent and ice sheet mean Antarctica; North Pole and ocean mean Arctic.

New Zealand

  • Identify: New Zealand is an island country of Oceania, and the southernmost country of the region.
  • Locate: It lies in the south-western Pacific, south-east of Australia across the Tasman Sea. Its two main islands are the North Island and the South Island, separated by Cook Strait.
  • Explain: Wellington, at the southern tip of the North Island, is the capital; Auckland, farther north, is the largest city. The Southern Alps run down the South Island, with Aoraki or Mount Cook as the highest peak. The country was settled by Maori voyagers from Polynesia before Europeans arrived, and the Treaty of Waitangi (see Terms) was signed in 1840.
  • Significance: "Southernmost country of Oceania" is the guide's exact superlative, and New Zealand is the default answer for Maori, kiwi bird and Southern Alps clues.
  • Connections: Links to Waitangi, to Australia across the Tasman Sea, and to the Cook Islands, which are in free association with it.
  • Confusables: Australia is bigger, richer in desert clues, and farther north and west; its capital is Canberra. Wellington, Auckland, Maori and the two-island split all point to New Zealand.

European Geography

The Bosporus

  • Identify: The Bosporus is the strait that divides the Asian and European parts of Turkey.
  • Locate: It runs through the city of Istanbul, joining the Black Sea in the north to the Sea of Marmara in the south.
  • Explain: It is about 30 kilometres long and in places less than a kilometre wide, crossed by three suspension bridges and an undersea rail tunnel. Together with the Sea of Marmara and the Dardanelles, it forms the Turkish Straits, the only water route between the Black Sea and the Mediterranean - a route governed by the Montreux Convention and vital to Ukraine, Russia and every other Black Sea state.
  • Significance: It is the world's classic continent-dividing strait and the hinge between the Black Sea entry below and Istanbul in the Asian section.
  • Connections: Black Sea to Bosporus to Sea of Marmara to Dardanelles to Aegean Sea to Mediterranean Sea - learn that chain as one unit.
  • Confusables: Bosporus vs Dardanelles. The Bosporus is the northern strait, at Istanbul, joining the Black Sea to Marmara. The Dardanelles is the southern strait, at Gallipoli and ancient Troy, joining Marmara to the Aegean. Istanbul in the clue means Bosporus; Gallipoli or Troy means Dardanelles.

The Danube and Vienna

  • Identify: The Danube is the second-longest river in Europe, and capital cities such as Vienna sit on it.
  • Locate: It rises in Germany's Black Forest and flows south-east for about 2,850 kilometres through or past Austria, Slovakia, Hungary, Croatia, Serbia, Bulgaria, Romania, Moldova and Ukraine, ending in a delta on the Black Sea.
  • Explain: No other river on Earth passes through so many countries or so many capitals: Vienna (Austria), Bratislava (Slovakia), Budapest (Hungary) and Belgrade (Serbia) all stand on it. It was the Roman empire's northern frontier and has been a trade corridor ever since.
  • Significance: "Second-longest river in Europe" plus "several capitals on one river" is a combination only the Danube satisfies.
  • Connections: Links to its Black Sea delta in Romania, to Austria and Vienna, and to the Rhine, its great western counterpart.
  • Confusables: The Volga is Europe's longest river, but it flows entirely within Russia and passes no foreign capitals - longest means Volga, second-longest and multi-capital means Danube. The Rhine flows north to Rotterdam and passes no national capital on its main stem.

Rome: the Trevi Fountain and the Colosseum

  • Identify: Rome is the capital of Italy, and its best-known tourist landmarks include the Trevi Fountain and the Colosseum.
  • Locate: Rome stands on the Tiber River in the Lazio region of west-central Italy, a short distance inland from the Tyrrhenian Sea. It entirely surrounds Vatican City, an independent state.
  • Explain: The Colosseum, opened in 80 CE, is the largest ancient amphitheatre ever built. The Trevi Fountain, finished in 1762, is the city's grandest Baroque fountain; tradition says a coin thrown into it ensures a return to Rome.
  • Significance: Rome is the default answer for ancient-empire, Colosseum and Vatican questions, and the two named attractions are the guide's chosen fingerprints.
  • Connections: Links to Naples and Vesuvius to the south, to Sicily, and to the Tiber as Italy's famous river.
  • Confusables: Naples is the large southern city in the shadow of Vesuvius - see its Terms entry. Venice is the canal city in the north-east, Florence the Renaissance city in Tuscany. Amphitheatre or Trevi clues can only be Rome.

The Black Sea and Crimea

  • Identify: The Black Sea is a large inland sea of strategic importance, and the Crimean Peninsula projects into its northern shore.
  • Locate: It is bordered by six countries: Ukraine, Russia and Georgia to the north and east, Turkey to the south, and Bulgaria and Romania to the west. Its only outlet is the Bosporus at its south-western corner.
  • Explain: Crimea is joined to mainland Ukraine by the narrow Isthmus of Perekop and faces Russia across the Kerch Strait, which links the Black Sea to the small Sea of Azov. Russia annexed Crimea in 2014, a move most of the world does not recognize, and the sea carries the grain and oil exports of ports such as Odesa and Novorossiysk.
  • Significance: "Strategically important body of water bordered by Crimea" is the guide's own wording, and the sea's single narrow outlet makes it a favourite chokepoint question.
  • Connections: Links to the Bosporus chain above, to the Kerch Strait and Sea of Azov, and to the Danube delta on its western shore.
  • Confusables: The Caspian Sea, farther east, is the world's largest lake and has no outlet to any ocean - a Bosporus outlet means Black Sea. The Sea of Azov is the Black Sea's shallow northern arm, not the main sea.

Sicily

  • Identify: Sicily is the largest island in the Mediterranean Sea.
  • Locate: It is an autonomous region of Italy, separated from the toe of the Italian mainland by the narrow Strait of Messina.
  • Explain: Its capital is Palermo, and Mount Etna on its eastern coast is one of the world's most active volcanoes. On a map Sicily is the triangular "football" at the toe of Italy's boot. Its position at the centre of the Mediterranean made it a prize for Greeks, Romans, Arabs, Normans and Spanish rulers in turn.
  • Significance: "Largest island in the Mediterranean" is a fixed superlative with a single answer.
  • Connections: Links to Italy and Rome, to Etna, and to Stromboli (see Terms), one of the Aeolian Islands just north of Sicily.
  • Confusables: Sardinia, west of Sicily, is the Mediterranean's second-largest island and also Italian. Cyprus is third and lies far to the east. Largest means Sicily; second-largest means Sardinia.

The Iberian Peninsula

  • Identify: The Iberian Peninsula is the south-western peninsula of Europe, home to Spain and Portugal.
  • Locate: It is cut off from the rest of Europe by the Pyrenees mountains along its north-eastern edge, and washed by the Atlantic Ocean to the west and north and the Mediterranean Sea to the south and east.
  • Explain: Spain occupies most of the peninsula, with its capital at Madrid in the centre; Portugal holds the western strip, with its capital at Lisbon on the Tagus estuary. The tiny principality of Andorra sits in the Pyrenees and British Gibraltar guards the southern strait, so "two countries" in the guide means the two large ones.
  • Significance: Peninsula-plus-two-countries is a standard pairing question, and Iberia is also the base for Spain-Portugal comparison questions.
  • Connections: Links to the Strait of Gibraltar at its foot, to the Pyrenees, and to Andalusia in its south.
  • Confusables: The Italian Peninsula (the boot) holds Italy, and the Balkan Peninsula holds Greece and its neighbours. Spain plus Portugal can only be Iberian.

Oslo and Akershus Fortress

  • Identify: Oslo is the capital city of Norway, and Akershus Fortress is its harbour fortress.
  • Locate: Oslo sits at the head of Oslofjord in south-eastern Norway. Akershus stands on a headland beside the harbour, a short walk from the city centre.
  • Explain: The fortress was begun in the late 13th century under King Haakon V, when the capital moved to Oslo, and it later served as a royal residence, a prison and a military headquarters; it withstood every siege laid against it. Today it hosts state ceremonies and museums. Oslo itself is one of Europe's fastest-growing capitals and home to the Nobel Peace Prize ceremony at City Hall.
  • Significance: The guide names the fortress rather than the city, which is the tell: questions may ask for either, so learn the pair as one unit.
  • Connections: Links to Norway's fjord coast, to Stockholm (Sweden) and Copenhagen (Denmark) as the other Scandinavian capitals, and to the Schengen Area in the Terms.
  • Confusables: Bergen is Norway's second city and old Hanseatic port on the west coast. Capital, fjord-head and fortress clues mean Oslo; if the fortress itself is the answer line, the answer is Akershus Fortress.

U.S. and Canadian Geography

New Orleans

  • Identify: New Orleans is a city in Louisiana famous for Cajun and Creole cuisine and for its annual Mardi Gras celebrations.
  • Locate: It stands on the Mississippi River in south-eastern Louisiana, between the river and Lake Pontchartrain, a short distance upriver from the Gulf of Mexico.
  • Explain: Founded by the French in 1718, its French Quarter, jazz tradition and Mardi Gras carnival are its signature clues. Much of the city lies below sea level between levees, which is why Hurricane Katrina's flooding in 2005 was so devastating.
  • Significance: Mardi Gras plus Cajun food is a two-clue fingerprint no other American city shares.
  • Connections: Links to the Mississippi River, to Louisiana, and to the Gulf of Mexico ports in the trainer bank.
  • Confusables: Baton Rouge is Louisiana's capital, upriver and inland - capitals questions want Baton Rouge, festival and food questions want New Orleans. Mobile, Alabama also holds Mardi Gras celebrations, but the Cajun cuisine clue settles it for New Orleans.

Baffin Island

  • Identify: Baffin Island is the largest island of Canada.
  • Locate: It lies in Nunavut in the Canadian Arctic, across Davis Strait and Baffin Bay from Greenland, with Hudson Strait to its south.
  • Explain: At over 500,000 square kilometres it is also the fifth-largest island in the world. Its main community is Iqaluit, the capital of Nunavut. It is named for the English explorer William Baffin.
  • Significance: "Largest island of Canada" is a clean superlative, and Baffin is the gateway answer for Nunavut questions.
  • Connections: Links to Nunavut and Iqaluit, to Greenland across the strait, and to Victoria and Ellesmere islands, Canada's next-largest.
  • Confusables: Greenland is the world's largest island but belongs to Denmark, not Canada. Victoria Island is Canada's second-largest and Ellesmere its third - largest in Canada means Baffin.

California's Moving Plates: Los Angeles and San Francisco

  • Identify: The guide's fact is that tectonic plate activity under California is slowly closing the distance between Los Angeles and San Francisco.
  • Locate: Los Angeles sits on the Pacific Plate, west of the San Andreas Fault; San Francisco sits mainly on the North American Plate, east of it.
  • Explain: The Pacific Plate is grinding north-west past the North American Plate along the San Andreas Fault at a few centimetres a year - roughly the speed fingernails grow. Los Angeles rides the Pacific side, so over millions of years it is creeping toward San Francisco; in about 15 million years the two could sit side by side. The same sideways (transform) motion stores the strain released in California's earthquakes.
  • Significance: This is the one plate-tectonics fact in the guide, and its answer line is the San Andreas Fault - or the Pacific Plate, if the question asks what Los Angeles sits on.
  • Connections: Links to the San Andreas Fault card in the bank, to Death Valley's rift basin, and to earthquake questions generally.
  • Confusables: The San Andreas is a transform boundary, where plates slide sideways - not a subduction zone like the Cascadia zone off Oregon and Washington, and not a spreading centre like the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. Sideways motion closing a distance between two cities means transform, and in California that means San Andreas.

The Great Lakes and Lake Superior

  • Identify: The Great Lakes are the five great freshwater lakes of North America: Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie and Ontario.
  • Locate: They sit on and near the border between Canada and the United States, draining north-east through the St. Lawrence River to the Atlantic. Lake Michigan is the only one lying entirely within the United States.
  • Explain: Together they hold about a fifth of the world's fresh surface water. Lake Superior is the largest of them and the largest freshwater lake in the world by surface area; it is also the deepest and northernmost of the five. The names can be remembered in order with the mnemonic HOMES - Huron, Ontario, Michigan, Erie, Superior - though the west-to-east order is Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie, Ontario. The Apostle Islands (see Terms) lie in Superior off Wisconsin, and the Niagara Escarpment and Niagara Falls belong to the Erie-Ontario end of the system.
  • Significance: Lake Superior's superlatives are the guide's chosen fact, and the five-lake system is the single most tested topic in North American physical geography.
  • Connections: Links to the St. Lawrence River, to the Apostle Islands, to Niagara, and to Baffin Island's Hudson Bay drainage farther north.
  • Confusables: Lake Superior superlatives, stated precisely: largest freshwater lake in the world by surface area; largest of the Great Lakes by both area and volume. But the Caspian Sea is larger overall - it is saline, so it does not take the freshwater title. Lake Baikal in Russia holds more fresh water by volume and is the world's deepest lake, but its surface area is smaller than Superior's. If a clue says "largest freshwater lake by area," buzz Superior. If it says "deepest" or "most water by volume," buzz Baikal.

The Rocky Mountains

  • Identify: The Rocky Mountains are the longest mountain system in North America.
  • Locate: They run from British Columbia and Alberta in Canada south through Montana, Idaho, Wyoming, Utah, Colorado and into New Mexico - a spine down the continent's western interior.
  • Explain: The Rockies form the Continental Divide: rivers west of it flow to the Pacific, rivers east of it to the Atlantic or the Gulf of Mexico. Their highest peak is Mount Elbert in Colorado, and parks such as Banff, Jasper and Rocky Mountain National Park protect their scenery.
  • Significance: "Longest mountain system in North America" is the guide's superlative, and the Rockies are the default answer for Continental Divide questions.
  • Connections: Links to the Andes, the world's longest system overall, and to the Appalachians, North America's older eastern range.
  • Confusables: The Appalachians are older, lower and heavily eroded, in the east. The Sierra Nevada and Coast Mountains are separate western ranges. Longest system and Continental Divide mean the Rockies.

Newfoundland and Labrador

  • Identify: The island of Newfoundland joins the mainland region of Labrador to form the Canadian province of Newfoundland and Labrador.
  • Locate: It is Canada's easternmost province: the island sits at the mouth of the Gulf of St. Lawrence, and Labrador lies on the mainland to the north-west, across the Strait of Belle Isle.
  • Explain: The capital and largest city is St. John's, on the island's Avalon Peninsula - one of the oldest European settlements in North America and the continent's easternmost city. Labrador borders Quebec and holds the Churchill River and its great hydroelectric station at Churchill Falls. The province joined Canada in 1949, the last province to do so.
  • Significance: The island-plus-mainland pairing is unique among provinces, and the guide flags it directly.
  • Connections: Links to the Atlantic provinces (Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Prince Edward Island), to the Gulf of St. Lawrence, and to St. John's as a frequent answer in its own right.
  • Confusables: St. John's (Newfoundland) vs Saint John (New Brunswick) - the apostrophe and the province separate them. And the province's name is the full "Newfoundland and Labrador," not Newfoundland alone, since the name was formally extended in 2001.

Death Valley

  • Identify: Death Valley is a desert valley in California whose national park once recorded the world's hottest temperature.
  • Locate: It lies in eastern California, in the Mojave Desert country near the Nevada border, within Death Valley National Park.
  • Explain: The 56.7 degrees Celsius (134 degrees Fahrenheit) reading at Furnace Creek in July 1913 remains the highest air temperature reliably recorded on Earth. Badwater Basin in the valley, at 86 metres below sea level, is the lowest point in North America. The valley is a graben, a block dropped between fault lines, and Mount Whitney, the highest point in the contiguous United States, stands in the same desert country.
  • Significance: It holds two superlatives at once - world's hottest recorded temperature and North America's lowest point - so it can be asked from either direction.
  • Connections: Links to Mount Whitney (highest in the contiguous United States), to California's tectonics above, and to the Mojave and Sonoran deserts.
  • Confusables: Laguna del Carbon in Argentina is the lowest point of the Americas overall, but North America's lowest is Badwater Basin. The Lut Desert in Iran and Death Valley dispute "hottest place" by satellite land-surface readings, but the official air-temperature record belongs to Furnace Creek.

Cape Cod and Martha's Vineyard

  • Identify: Cape Cod is the hooked peninsula of Massachusetts, and it is located across from the island of Martha's Vineyard.
  • Locate: The cape curls into the Atlantic from the Massachusetts mainland; Martha's Vineyard lies just south of it, across Vineyard Sound, with Nantucket island farther east.
  • Explain: Cape Cod was named for the codfish once caught in huge numbers off its shores, and the Pilgrims first landed at its tip, at Provincetown, in 1620 before settling at Plymouth. Martha's Vineyard holds the towns of Edgartown, Oak Bluffs and Vineyard Haven and is a famous summer colony reached by ferry from Woods Hole on the cape. The cape, the Vineyard and Nantucket together form Massachusetts's classic vacation geography.
  • Significance: The guide pairs the cape and the island in one fact, so a question may ask for either side of the pairing.
  • Connections: Links to Massachusetts, to Long Island across the sound to the south-west, and to Mystic, Connecticut (see Terms) along the same New England coast.
  • Confusables: Cape Ann is the smaller cape north of Boston. Nantucket is the island east of Martha's Vineyard, farther offshore and historically a whaling port. Across-the-water-from-Cape-Cod means Martha's Vineyard.

Charleston

  • Identify: Charleston is the largest city by population in South Carolina, set on its namesake harbour.
  • Locate: Charleston Harbor lies on South Carolina's Atlantic coast, where the Ashley and Cooper rivers meet.
  • Explain: Founded in 1670 as Charles Towne, the city was colonial America's rich port for rice and indigo and a major entry point of the transatlantic slave trade; the first shots of the Civil War were fired at Fort Sumter in its harbour in 1861. Its preserved historic district is its modern fingerprint.
  • Significance: "Largest city in South Carolina" is a useful trap-setter, because the capital is elsewhere - exactly why the guide names the fact.
  • Connections: Links to Fort Sumter, to the Atlantic coast, and to Savannah, Georgia, its sister port to the south.
  • Confusables: Columbia is South Carolina's capital and second city, inland at the state's centre. Largest city means Charleston; capital means Columbia. Charlotte is larger still, but it is in North Carolina.

Latin American and Caribbean Geography

Honduras and Tegucigalpa

  • Identify: Honduras is a Central American country whose capital is Tegucigalpa.
  • Locate: It borders Guatemala to the west, El Salvador to the south-west and Nicaragua to the south-east, with a long Caribbean coast to the north and a short Pacific coast on the Gulf of Fonseca.
  • Explain: The guide's border set - El Salvador and Nicaragua - plus the capital is the fingerprint; Guatemala completes the border list. Tegucigalpa sits in a highland valley in the country's mountainous interior, which is why Honduras's main Caribbean port, San Pedro Sula's neighbour Puerto Cortes, handles most trade. The Bay Islands, including Roatan, lie off its Caribbean coast, and the Maya ruins of Copan stand near its western border.
  • Significance: Central America's seven countries and their capitals are asked as a set, and Honduras-Tegucigalpa is the pairing students most often fumble.
  • Connections: Links to Belize (north of Guatemala), to Nicaragua and Lake Nicaragua to the south, and to the Caribbean entries below.
  • Confusables: Nicaragua is the larger neighbour to the south-east, capital Managua. El Salvador is the small Pacific neighbour, capital San Salvador. The hard-to-spell capital, Tegucigalpa, belongs to Honduras alone.

The Perito Moreno Glacier

  • Identify: The Perito Moreno Glacier is a great glacier of Argentine Patagonia.
  • Locate: It lies in Los Glaciares National Park in Santa Cruz province, in the southern Andes of Argentina, near the town of El Calafate.
  • Explain: The glacier is about 30 kilometres long and ends in a towering ice wall in Lake Argentino, where huge blocks calve into the water with a roar - the spectacle that makes it one of Argentina's top tourist sites. Unlike most of the world's glaciers, it has stayed roughly in balance rather than retreating fast. It is named for Francisco Moreno, the Argentine explorer and boundary expert nicknamed "Perito," meaning expert.
  • Significance: It is the Southern Patagonian Ice Field's most famous outlet glacier and the guide's chosen Argentine landmark.
  • Connections: Links to Patagonia, to Lake Argentino, and across the border to Chile's Torres del Paine National Park.
  • Confusables: Torres del Paine is in Chile, not Argentina. Upsala is the larger glacier in the same Argentine park but lacks the famous calving wall facing a town-side viewpoint. Ice wall plus Lake Argentino plus El Calafate means Perito Moreno.

Lake Titicaca

  • Identify: Lake Titicaca is the largest lake in South America by surface area.
  • Locate: It straddles the border of Peru and Bolivia, high in the Andes on the Altiplano at about 3,812 metres above sea level.
  • Explain: That altitude also makes it the highest navigable lake in the world. The Peruvian city of Puno is its main port, and the Uru people live on floating islands woven from totora reeds.
  • Significance: It carries two superlatives at once - largest in South America by area, highest navigable in the world - so it is asked twice as often as other lakes.
  • Connections: Links to Peru and Bolivia, to the Altiplano, and to La Paz, which sits in a canyon below the lake's plain.
  • Confusables: Lake Maracaibo in Venezuela is sometimes called South America's largest lake, but it is a brackish tidal inlet connected to the sea, not a true freshwater lake - the "largest lake" title belongs to Titicaca. And "largest by area" is the guide's wording: by volume Titicaca also leads among South American lakes, so either framing lands on the same answer.

Cuba and Havana

  • Identify: Cuba is a country in the Caribbean which has its capital at Havana.
  • Locate: Cuba is the largest island of the Caribbean, lying just south of Florida across the Straits of Florida, west of Hispaniola and north of Jamaica.
  • Explain: Havana, founded by the Spanish in 1519, grew into the great harbour of the Spanish Caribbean; its old town, Malecon seawall and Morro Castle are its fingerprints. Cuba stretches over 1,200 kilometres end to end.
  • Significance: Largest Caribbean island plus its capital is a foundational pairing for the whole region's geography.
  • Connections: Links to the Cayman Islands and Jamaica to its south, to Guantanamo Bay at its eastern end, and to Miami and Little Havana across the straits.
  • Confusables: Hispaniola is the second-largest Caribbean island and is shared by Haiti and the Dominican Republic. Cuba is one country on one island. Puerto Rico is a US territory far to the east. Largest island and Havana both mean Cuba.

Terms for Further Study

The guide lists these terms without explanation and tells students to research them. Note that its printed numbering repeats 21-23, so the list holds 33 distinct terms, not 30. Each entry below gives the identification, the location, and the trap to avoid.

  1. Geyser - A hot spring that periodically erupts, jetting hot water and steam skyward as underground water flashes to steam; Old Faithful in Yellowstone and Strokkur in Iceland are the classic examples. Trap: a fumarole vents only steam, with no water.
  2. Riyadh - Capital and largest city of Saudi Arabia, set inland on the Najd plateau at the centre of the Arabian Peninsula. Trap: Jeddah is the Red Sea port and Mecca the holy city; neither is the capital.
  3. Micronesia - A subregion of Oceania: thousands of small islands scattered across the western Pacific, north of Melanesia. It also names the Federated States of Micronesia, capital Palikir. Trap: Polynesia lies farther east, Melanesia south.
  4. Sahel - The semiarid transition belt immediately south of the Sahara, running from Senegal to Sudan, where desert grades into savanna. Trap: the Sahara is the desert itself, north of the Sahel.
  5. Conflict minerals - Minerals whose sale finances armed groups, classically the 3TG set - tin, tantalum, tungsten and gold - plus cobalt, mined in and around the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Trap: blood diamonds are the gem-specific case.
  6. Schengen Area - The zone of 29 European countries that have abolished internal border checks, allowing passport-free travel; mostly EU states plus Norway, Switzerland and others. Trap: the eurozone is about currency, not borders.
  7. Bryce Canyon National Park - A Utah park famous for hoodoos, thin orange-pink rock spires eroded from a plateau rim; despite the name it is an amphitheatre, not a canyon. Trap: the Grand Canyon is Arizona's far larger true canyon.
  8. Escarpment - A long, steep slope or cliff separating two fairly level areas, often where hard rock caps softer rock; the Niagara Escarpment by the Great Lakes is the standard example. Trap: a cuesta has one steep face and one gentle dip slope.
  9. Red River (Asia) - The river, also called the Hong River, that rises in China's Yunnan province, flows through Hanoi in Vietnam, and spreads into a delta entering the Gulf of Tonkin. Trap: see the confusable box below.
  10. Arizona - The US Southwest state whose capital is Phoenix, holding the Grand Canyon in its north and much of the Sonoran Desert in its south. Trap: New Mexico is its eastern neighbour; both are desert states, but only Arizona holds the Grand Canyon.
  11. Somalia - The Horn of Africa country with Africa's longest mainland coastline, on the Gulf of Aden and Indian Ocean; capital Mogadishu, east of Ethiopia. Trap: Ethiopia, its neighbour, has been landlocked since Eritrea's independence.
  12. Osaka - Japan's great merchant port city on Osaka Bay, in the Kansai region of Honshu, long famed for commerce and food. Trap: Kyoto, the old imperial capital, is its smaller inland neighbour.
  13. Stromboli - A small volcanic island in the Aeolian group north of Sicily, in Italy's Tyrrhenian Sea; its volcano erupts so regularly it is nicknamed the Lighthouse of the Mediterranean. Trap: Etna is the huge volcano on Sicily itself.
  14. Hollywood - A district of Los Angeles, California, synonymous with the American film industry and marked by the Hollywood Sign in the hills. Trap: Bollywood is Mumbai's film industry in India.
  15. Estuary - The tidal lower reach of a river, where fresh water mixes with sea water - Chesapeake Bay and the Thames at London are examples. Trap: a delta is sediment built at a river's mouth; an estuary is the drowned, tidal mouth itself.
  16. Arkansas - The US South state whose capital and largest city is Little Rock, on the Arkansas River, bordering the Mississippi to the east. Trap: Kansas lies to the north-west; Arkansas is pronounced AR-kan-saw.
  17. Paseo de la Reforma - Mexico City's grand diagonal avenue, modelled on European boulevards, running past the Angel of Independence column toward Chapultepec Park. Trap: the Zocalo is the main square, not the avenue.
  18. Saint Kitts and Nevis - A two-island federation in the Caribbean's Leeward Islands, the smallest sovereign state in the Americas by both area and population; capital Basseterre, on Saint Kitts. Trap: Saint Lucia and Saint Vincent are separate states farther south.
  19. Strait of Hormuz - The narrow chokepoint between Iran and Oman joining the Persian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman, carrying a large share of the world's seaborne oil. Trap: the Bab el-Mandeb guards the Red Sea, not the Gulf.
  20. Apostle Islands - An island group in Lake Superior off northern Wisconsin, protected as a national lakeshore and famed for red sandstone sea caves and winter ice caves. Trap: Isle Royale is the large island national park farther north in the same lake.
  21. Waitangi - The Bay of Islands site in New Zealand where the Treaty of Waitangi was signed in 1840 between the British Crown and Maori chiefs, the country's founding document. Trap: Wellington is the capital; Waitangi is the treaty site.
  22. Naples - Southern Italy's great port city, on its bay in Campania, overshadowed by Mount Vesuvius, with ruined Pompeii nearby. Trap: Rome is the capital far to the north; Vesuvius in view means Naples.
  23. Kyber Pass - The guide's spelling of the Khyber Pass, the historic mountain route linking Pakistan's Peshawar valley with eastern Afghanistan - the classic invasion and trade corridor into South Asia. Trap: the Bolan Pass is Pakistan's other Afghan route, farther south.
  24. Flemish (people) - The Flemings are the Dutch-speaking people of Flanders, Belgium's northern region; their language, Flemish, is a variety of Dutch. Trap: the Walloons are Belgium's French-speaking southerners.
  25. Lake Turkana - The world's largest permanent desert lake, on the Kenya-Ethiopia border in arid northern Kenya, fed mainly by Ethiopia's Omo River; formerly called Lake Rudolf. Trap: Lake Victoria is the giant lake far to the south-west.
  26. Mystic, Connecticut - A historic seaport village in Connecticut, on the Mystic River by Long Island Sound, home to Mystic Seaport Museum and the preserved whaling ship Charles W. Morgan. Trap: Mystic is a village within Groton and Stonington, not a city.
  27. Kowloon Walled City - A single, extraordinarily dense Hong Kong city block - an almost ungoverned enclave housing tens of thousands - demolished in 1993-94 and replaced by a park. Trap: Kowloon is the whole peninsula district; the walled city was one block of it.
  28. Black sand beaches - Beaches whose sand forms from eroded dark volcanic rock, usually basalt lava; famous examples occur in Hawaii, Iceland and New Zealand. Trap: white sand is usually quartz or ground coral, not volcanic material.
  29. Congo rainforests - The rainforest of the Congo Basin in Central Africa, the world's second-largest tropical rainforest, spanning the Democratic Republic of the Congo and its neighbours along the Congo River. Trap: the Amazon is larger; second-largest means Congo.
  30. Oahu - The Hawaiian island holding Honolulu, the state capital, and Pearl Harbor; the state's most populous island. Trap: the island of Hawaii, the Big Island, is the largest; Honolulu means Oahu.
  31. Olympic Peninsula - Washington state's large north-western peninsula, between Puget Sound and the Pacific Ocean, holding Olympic National Park, its temperate rainforests and Mount Olympus. Trap: it lies across the sound from Seattle, not on the mainland side.
  32. McMurdo Station - The largest research station in Antarctica, an American base on Ross Island at the edge of the Ross Ice Shelf, supporting journeys to the South Pole. Trap: Amundsen-Scott Station stands at the Pole itself.
  33. Kangchenjunga - The world's third-highest mountain, 8,586 metres, on the Nepal-India border in the Himalayas; it is India's highest peak, in Sikkim. Trap: Everest is first and K2 second; third-highest is Kangchenjunga.

Confusable box: the two Red Rivers

  • Red River (Asia) - Also called the Hong River. Rises in Yunnan, China; flows south-east through Hanoi, Vietnam; builds a broad delta; empties into the Gulf of Tonkin. Clues: Hanoi, Tonkin, Yunnan, Hong.
  • Red River of the North - Forms the border between Minnesota and North Dakota in the United States, flows north through Winnipeg in Canada, and empties into Lake Winnipeg. Clues: Fargo, Grand Forks, Winnipeg, flowing north.
  • The shared trick: both are simply "the Red River" in their own regions. The continent decides. Asian clues - Hanoi, delta rice country, Tonkin - mean the Asian river. North American clues - state border, flowing north into Canada - mean the Red River of the North. A third river, the Red River of the South, forms part of the Texas-Oklahoma border and joins the Mississippi system; if Texas or Oklahoma appears, that is the one.

Final checklist before the Red bee

  1. Run the six guide sections once by superlative: longest, largest, tallest, third-longest - each one has exactly one right answer in this guide.
  2. Drill the straits chain in order: Black Sea, Bosporus, Sea of Marmara, Dardanelles, Aegean, Mediterranean.
  3. Drill the capital traps: Istanbul is not Turkey's capital, Lagos is not Nigeria's, Baton Rouge not New Orleans, Columbia not Charleston, Sucre not La Paz.
  4. Say the two Red Rivers and the Niger-Nigeria-Niger triangle out loud until the distinction is automatic.
